```
## Certification Requirements

### NRSWA (New Roads and Street Works Act 1991)
- **Unit 2**: Site Setting Out (required for all operatives working in or near carriageway)
- **Unit 3**: Signing, Lighting, Guarding
- **Unit 4-8**: Reinstatement (various materials)
- **Supervisor Units (1, 3, 10, 12)**: Required for supervisors
- Issued by: City & Guilds, NOCN, or equivalent approved body
- Renewal: every 5 years

### HDD / Directional Drilling
- **IADC (International Association of Drilling Contractors)**: Drill operator certification
- **Highway Authority approval**: Bore plan submission and approval before drilling
- **Network Rail Sentinel**: Required for works near railway infrastructure

### Fiber Optic
- **City & Guilds 3667**: Fiber Optic Communication Technology
  - Unit 201: Install and terminate structured cabling
  - Unit 207: Install and terminate fiber optic cabling
  - Unit 208: Test and fault-find fiber optic cabling
- **ECS/JIB Fiber Card**: Industry ID card for fiber professionals
- Operator must hold valid OTDR test equipment qualification

### Gas Proximity
- **Gas Proximity Working**: Required for all contractors working within 3m of gas main
- Issued by: National Grid Gas / Cadent authorized training providers
- Covers: Gas escape recognition, emergency procedures, safe dig requirements

### High Voltage Electricity
- **DNO Approved**: Distribution Network Operator (e.g., UK Power Networks) must approve all HV contractors
- **ECS Gold Card (HV Jointer)**: City & Guilds HV jointing qualification
- **NICEIC / ECA Registration**: Electrical contractor registration

### Confined Space
- **City & Guilds 6150**: Confined Space Safety
- Required for: chamber entry, manhole work, any space with restricted entry/exit
- Includes: entrant, standby person, and rescue team certification

### Asbestos Awareness
- **HSE Asbestos Awareness (Cat A)**: For all workers who may disturb asbestos
- Required in all demolition/excavation work on pre-2000 buildings/infrastructure
```

```
# Trenchless Installation Methods Reference

## Overview

Trenchless technology enables utility installation without continuous open-cut excavation.
Key benefit: minimal surface disruption, suitable for crossings under roads, railways, rivers, and buildings.

---

## 1. Horizontal Directional Drilling (HDD)

### What it is
A steerable drilling technique using a drill head guided underground to create a bore,
then pulling back the utility through the bored path.

### Typical Applications
- Road and motorway crossings
- River and waterway crossings
- Under railway lines
- Through congested utility zones
- Lengths: typically 20m–500m per bore

### Key Equipment
- HDD drill rig (sized by pull-back force: 5T to 1,000T+)
- Drill head with transmitter (walk-over or wire-line tracking)
- Drill rods and mud mixing unit
- Pull-back swivel and pull-head

### Certifications Required
- IADC (International Association of Drilling Contractors) operator certification
- Bore plan approval from Highway Authority / Network Rail / Canal & River Trust

### Process
1. Pilot bore: small diameter drill from launch to exit
2. Reaming: enlarge bore to 1.3–1.5× pipe/duct OD (multiple passes)
3. Pull-back: duct/pipe pulled from exit back to launch
4. Grouting: optional — fill annular space with bentonite grout

### Risks
- Inadvertent Returns (IR): drilling fluid breaking to surface
- Deviation: bore strays outside tolerance — requires re-drill
- Adjacent service damage: especially near plastic water/gas mains
- Settlement: avoid on structures with shallow foundations

### Typical Costs (UK, 2024)
- Small bore (<150mm OD, <50m): £4,000–£8,000 per crossing
- Medium bore (<300mm OD, 50–200m): £10,000–£35,000 per crossing
- Large bore: project-specific

---

## 2. Pipe Bursting

### What it is
Breaking an existing pipe (usually old iron or clay) and simultaneously pulling in a new pipe
of the same or larger diameter.

### Applications
- Water main replacement (iron to PE)
- Gas main replacement (iron to MDPE)
- Sewer replacement

### Key Equipment
- Hydraulic/pneumatic bursting head
- Pulling unit (rod-pull or winch)
- New pipe string ready for installation

### Not suitable for
- PVC or MDPE pipes (fracture unpredictably)
- Pipes with live connections that cannot be interrupted
- Where adjacent utilities are within 500mm

---

## 3. Micro-Trenching

### What it is
A narrow saw-cut trench (typically 40–80mm wide × 400mm deep) used to bury small cables/ducts
in footways or cycle lanes.

### Applications
- Fiber optic cables in microducts
- Small LV electricity cables
- Urban areas with congested underground

### Limitations
- Footway use only (not in carriageway — depth inadequate)
- Services must be mapped before sawing
- Ground radar survey mandatory
- Not suitable in areas with shallow buried services

### Typical Costs
- £25–£45 per metre (footway)
- Much less disruption than open cut

---

## 4. Moling (Impact Moling)

### What it is
A pneumatic or hydraulic mole (torpedo) that forces its way through undisturbed ground,
creating a bore without removing soil.

### Applications
- Service connections under footways (gas, water, electricity, fiber)
- Short crossings: typically 5–30m
- Soft, cohesive soils

### Limitations
- Cannot be steered (straight runs only)
- Avoid near existing services due to displacement risk
- Rock or gravel: not suitable

---

## 5. Open Cut (Conventional Trenching)

### What it is
Traditional excavation of a trench, laying the utility, and backfilling/reinstating.

### When it's used
- Where trenchless is not economical or not feasible
- Large diameter pipes (>600mm)
- Complex junction works
- Repair and maintenance

### Key Requirements
- NRSWA streetworks notice
- Traffic Management plan (TTRO for A-roads: 3-month notice)
- Safe dig near existing services (CAT, Genny, GPR, trial holes)
- Phased excavation recommended in congested zones

### Costs
- Approximately £200–£800/m depending on depth, ground conditions, traffic impact, and reinstatement type

---

## Method Selection Guide

| Scenario | Recommended Method |
|---|---|
| Road crossing, no open cut permitted | HDD |
| Footway, small fiber cable | Micro-trenching |
| Short service connection | Moling |
| Replacing old iron/clay pipe | Pipe Bursting |
| Large diameter main, complex junction | Open Cut |
| Existing reserved duct available | Cable Blowing (no excavation) |
```

```
# Utility Installation Standards Reference
## Source: NJUG Volume 1 (UK) + Industry Standards

## Minimum Separation Requirements Between Utilities

All separations measured between **outer surfaces** of assets.

| Utility 1 | Utility 2 | Minimum Separation |
|---|---|---|
| Gas (any pressure) | Water main | 300mm |
| Gas (IP/HP) | Electricity (HV) | 500mm |
| Gas (IP/HP) | Electricity (LV) | 250mm |
| Gas (any) | Fiber/Comms | 250mm |
| Gas (any) | Drainage/Sewer | 500mm |
| Electricity (HV) | Water main | 250mm |
| Electricity (LV) | Water main | 150mm |
| Electricity | Fiber/Comms | 100mm |
| Water | Drainage/Sewer | 250mm |
| Fiber/Comms | Drainage/Sewer | 100mm |

## Recommended Minimum Depths (top of asset to surface)

### Electricity
- LV (up to 1kV): 450mm footway, 600mm carriageway
- HV (1kV–33kV): 900mm footway, 900mm carriageway
- Note: DNO may specify greater depth — always check

### Gas
- Low Pressure (LP, <75mbar): 375mm, 750mm near buildings
- Intermediate Pressure (IP, 75mbar–2bar): 600mm
- Medium Pressure (MP, 2bar–7bar): 600mm
- High Pressure (HP, >7bar): 900mm

### Water
- Service pipes (<50mm): 750mm
- Distribution mains (50–300mm): 750mm carriageway, 900mm recommended
- Trunk mains (>300mm): 900mm minimum

### Fiber / Telecommunications
- Footway: 350mm (minimum), 450mm (recommended)
- Carriageway: 600mm (minimum)
- In duct: add duct wall thickness to above

### Drainage / Sewers
- Min 600mm cover to invert (top of pipe)
- Shallow drains (<600mm): mark with mesh marker tape
- Deep sewers: structural design required

## Marker Tape Requirements
- All services require marker tape 150mm above the asset
- Gas: yellow tape "DANGER GAS PIPE BELOW"
- Water: blue tape
- Electricity: red tape "DANGER ELECTRIC CABLE BELOW"
- Fiber: black tape or no standard color

## Duct Standards for Future Reservation

### Fiber/Comms Ducts
- Minimum 110mm HDPE (allows 96-core SMF via cable blowing)
- 63mm ducts acceptable for small cable runs
- Draw rope mandatory for all reserved ducts
- Entry/exit chambers at maximum 500m intervals

### Power Ducts
- 150mm HDPE for cables up to 185mm²
- 200mm for cables up to 300mm² or bundled circuits
- Sand bed required below, concrete haunching optional

### Documentation
Every installed duct must be:
1. Recorded in LSBUD / NUAR database
2. Given unique asset ID
3. Photographed at installation (entry, exit, mid-point)
4. As-built survey completed to ±150mm accuracy
```
